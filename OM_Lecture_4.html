Strategies in a repeated game

  • How to analyse such a game?
  • A strategy is a plan for the whole game
  • Many strategies!
  • We consider a very simple one: Grim Trigger
  • Cooperate until someone doesn't, then punish forever
  • Punish = short term optimal (Shirk/ No Bonus)

Grim Trigger: Deviations

  • Pay-off Principal if everone cooperates:
  • $\Pi_P=1+\delta\cdot 1+\delta^2\cdot 1+\ldots$
  • $\Pi_P=1+\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}$
  • What happens if the principal deviates?
  • He gets 3 now, then 0 forever
  • Deviation not attractive if $1+\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\ge 3 \iff \delta\ge \dfrac 23$
  • Similar analysis for agent
  • Cooperate if you care enough about the future and expect others to cooperate

General Idea Grim Trigger

  • $\Pi^C$: pay-off cooperation
  • $\Pi^D$: pay-off one time (unilateral) deviation
  • $\Pi^N$: pay-off no cooperation
  • Cooperation: $\Pi^C+\delta \Pi^C+\ldots= \Pi^C +\Pi^C\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}$
  • Deviation: $\Pi^D + \delta \Pi^N +\ldots= \Pi^D+\Pi^N\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}$
  • Cooperate if: $\Pi^C +\Pi^C\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\ge \Pi^D+\Pi^N\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta} \iff \delta \ge \dfrac{\Pi^D-\Pi^C}{\Pi^D-\Pi^N}$

Feasibility of cooperation

  • When is cooperation feasible?
  • $\delta \ge \dfrac{\Pi^D-\Pi^C}{\Pi^D-\Pi^N}$
  • If $\delta\uparrow$
  • If $\Pi^C\uparrow$
  • If $\Pi^N\downarrow$
  • $\delta \ge \dfrac{\Pi^D-\Pi^C}{\Pi^D-\Pi^N}=1-\dfrac{\Pi^C-\Pi^N}{\Pi^D-\Pi^N}$
  • If $\Pi^D\downarrow$
  • We try to understand many strategic situation by analysing these features

Relational Contracts

  • Simple bonus game: repition allowed effort
  • More complex question: how close can we come to optimal effort?
  • When a principal offer a contract to induce effort, that affects his $\Pi^C$, $\Pi^D$ and (perhaps) his $\Pi^N$
  • What wins?
  • Bull's model: analysis of simple case
  • Lesson: inducing optimal effort is harder (for the principal!) than inducing some effort

Bull's Model

  • Risk neutral Principal and Agent
  • Agent can carry out risky project
    • A movie that can be a hit or a flop
    • A new product that can sell well or not
  • Assume only 2 outcomes:
    • Hit: Principal receives $v$ ($0\lt v \lt1$)
    • Flop: Principal receives 0

Timeline

  1. Principal offers compensation package $(w,\beta)$
    • Agent receives $w$ if flop
    • Agent receives $w+\beta$ if hit
  2. Agents accepts or rejects offer
    • If rejects, round ends, Agent receives $U_{out}$ and Principal receives nothing
  3. Agent chooses effort $e\in[0,1]$
    • Cost of effort $C(e)=\frac 12 e^2$
    • Probability of hit $P=e$
  4. If hit, Principals chooses whether to honour bonus

One-Shot Equilibrium

  1. Principal does not pay bonus
  2. Agent exerts zero effort
  3. Agent accepts only if $w\ge U_{out}$
  4. Principal sets $w\lt U_{out}$

Not efficient!

Contract pay-offs

  • Suppose Agent believed contract $(w,\beta)$
  • $\Pi_A=w+\beta e-\frac 12 e^2$
  • Agent chooses $e=\beta$ and has utility $\Pi_A=w+\frac 12 \beta^2$
  • Principal considers all contracts $(w,\beta)$
  • Principal would never pay more than $U_{out}$, so $w=U_{out}-\frac 12 \beta^2$
  • Given that agent exerts $e=\beta$:
  • $\Pi_P(\beta)=e (v-\beta) - w= \beta(v-\beta)-U_{out}+\frac 12 \beta^2= \frac{(2v-\beta)\beta}2-U_{out}$
  • Optimal contract would be $\beta=v$

Cooperation?

  • If Principals sticks to contract, $\Pi^C=\frac{(2v-\beta)\beta}2-U_{out}$
  • If he deviates, gains $\Pi^D=\beta$ now
  • But cooperation ceases and nothing is produced after: $\Pi^N=0$
  • Cooperation if $\delta \ge \dfrac{\Pi^D-\Pi^C}{\Pi^D-\Pi^N}$
  • Can rewrite it as $\Pi^C\ge (\frac 1\delta -1)\beta =r\beta$
  • (Here $r=\dfrac 1{1+\delta}$, the interest rate)

Collusion in Markets

  • Tacit vs explicit collusion
  • Explicit:
    • Independent firms take joint decisions
    • Adam Smith: "people of the same trade seldom meet together (...), but the conversation ends in a conspiracy against the public"
  • Implicit:
    • Independent firms take independent decisions
    • that have the same consequences as in the situation in which the decisions would have been taken jointly

Why don't firms collude all the time?

  • Cartels are illegal
    • Competition law: Article 101 EU treaty
    • Cartel agreements are not enforceable in court
  • Incentives to deviate from a collusive agreement
    • Too low discount factor (short-term policy)
    • Sector specific factors that influence $\Pi^D$, $\Pi^C$ and $\Pi^N$.
    • Consider Cournot competition. What are $\Pi^D$, $\Pi^C$ and $\Pi^N$?
    • $\Pi_N$ is regular Cournot profit
    • $\Pi_C$ is monopoly profit, shared equally
    • $\Pi_D$ is profit from flooding the market alone

Sector Specfic Factors

  • Market Concentration
  • High Entry Barriers
  • Transparency
  • Frequent Interaction

Market Concentration

  • Suppose Bertrand competition, $n$ firms
  • $\Pi^N=0$
  • $\Pi^C=\Pi_M/n$
  • $\Pi^D\approx \Pi_M$
  • $\delta\ge\dfrac{\Pi^D-\Pi^C}{\Pi^D-\Pi^N}=\dfrac{\Pi_M-\frac{\Pi_m}n}{\Pi_M-0}=1-\dfrac 1n$

(Un)Stable Demand

  • If demand is stable
    • Easier to observe competitors
    • Easier to react to deviation
    • Facilitates collusion
  • If demand is unstable:
    • deviation is attractive if demand is high
    • deviation is not attractive if demand is low
    • Price wars in economic booms.

Facilitating Practices

  • Authorities may block:
  • Mergers
    • Lead to greater market concentration
    • Facilitate collusion
  • Lowest Price guarantees
  • Most-favoured-customer clauses
  • Both decrease $\Pi_D$
  • Deviations punished by contract
  • Again facilitates collusion
