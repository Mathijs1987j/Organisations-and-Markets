Principal-Agent Models

  • Principal has some project he wants carried out
  • Agent could carry it out
  • For example:
    • Employer-employee
    • Investor-entrepreneur
    • Voters-politician
  • Principal can usually not observe everything agent does:
    • Moral Hazard
  • Last week (not quite): work in teams

Basic Principal-Agent Model and Efficiency

  • Porfirio owns a machine. Operating the machine for $e$ hours produces $e$ units of a good with price $p$
  • Ambrosius can operate the machine, at a cost $\frac 12 e^2$ for $e$ hours of operation
  • What is an efficient allocation?
  • $V(e)=ep-\frac 12 e^2$
  • FOC: $p-e=0$ ⇒ $e=p$
  • Is efficient outcome unique?
    • Productive vs allocative efficiency
  • $V(e^*)=p^2-\frac 12 p^2=\frac 12 p^2$
  • Any outcome that yields that overall value is efficient, and only those

Principal Agent and the Market

  • Suppose both Porfirio and Ambrosius were price takers
  • They take $p$ as given, and $w$, the hourly wage
  • For Ambrosius: $U_A=we-\frac 12 e^2$ ⇒ FOC: $w-e=0$ ⇔ Supply: $e=w$
  • For Porfirio: $\Pi_P=e(p-w)$
  • If $p>w$, his demand for $e$ would be unbounded; if $p\lt w$, his demand would be $e=0$
  • Neither clears the market, as supply $\ne$ demand
  • So $p=w$ and Porfirio demands $e=w$, even though he is indifferent
  • Outcome is efficient, $\Pi_P=0$, $U_A=\frac 12 p^2$

Principal-Agent Model as Dynamic Game

  • Now suppose Porfirio offers a contract directly to Ambrosius
  • We will consider linear contracts:
    • Up-front payment of $w$ and per hour payment of $\beta$
    • (Notice that $\beta$ was $w$ before...)
  • Game with three stages
    1. $P$(orfirio) offers contract (choosing values $w$ and $\beta$)
    2. $A$(mbrosius) accepts or rejects

      • If $A$ rejects, $P$ gets 0 and $A$ gets $U_{out}$
      • If $A$ accepts, he chooses $e$, gets paid according to the contract and $P$ gets the rest of the profit

Optimal Contracts

  • In the last stage, $U_A(e)=w+\beta e-\frac 12 e^2$
    • FOC: $\beta-e=0$ ⇒ $e=\beta$
  • In the penultimate stage, $A$ accepts as long as $U_A\ge U_{out}$
  • In the first stage, $P$ can be clever in two ways:
    • If $P$ sets $\beta=p$, then (in the final stage) $e=p$ and production is efficient
      • (This works more generally: $\beta=p$ means the agent faces all the costs and benefits and thus maximizes efficiency)
    • If $P$ sets $U_A=U_{out}$, then he minimizes the payment to $A$
      • (This is also generally optimal: incentivise the agent just enough that he doesn't quit)
  • If $U_{out}=0$, then $0=U_A=w+p^2-\frac 12 p^2= \frac 12 p^2$
    • $P$ earns full surplus
    • Still efficient, complete reverse of market
    • Power of moving first (and proposing contract)
  • Some markets are like this: Taxi drivers pay lump sum fee, franchisees pay fixed rent

Team production

  • When many work on a project, individual contributions are unclear
  • Rewards are usually based on total performance
  • Leads to free-rider problem
  • Simple model of team production:
    • Costs and benefits measured in money
    • If team members exert efforts $e_i$, $i=1,2,\ldots, n$
    • Quantity (sold at price $p$) is $e_1+\ldots+e_n$
    • Costs (individual) are $c_i(e_i)=\frac 12 e_i^2$

Efficient outcome

  • The value created is $V(e_1,\ldots,e_n)=p\cdot(e_1+\ldots+e_n)-(\frac 12 e_1^2+\ldots+\frac 12 e_n^2$)
  • Maximizing this value w.r.t. $e_i$
  • FOC: $p-e_i=0$, or $e^*=p$
  • Is efficient outcome unique?
    • Productive vs allocative efficiency

Nash of Team

  • What is the Nash equilibrium if every employee decides efforts individually and they split profits equally?
  • $\Pi_i(e_1,\ldots,e_n)=\frac 1n p\cdot(e_1+\ldots+e_n)-\frac 12 e_i^2$
  • FOC: $\frac 1n p - e_i=0$, or $e_i=\frac pn$
  • (This is a dominant strategy; extreme simplification)
  • $e_i\lt e^*$, and it gets worse as $n\uparrow$
  • Free-rider problem: your effort benefits everyone, you only work for your slice
  • Very bad underproduction (positive externalities)
  • Potential solution could be tournament (see last week's slides)

Dynamic games in the market

Pricing in Vertical Chains

  • Vertical chain with upstream wholesale firm and downstream retailer
  • Both are monopolists
  • Two-stage game
    • Wholesaler sets wholesale price $p_W$
    • Retailer observes $p_W$ and sets retail price $p_R$
    • (Consumers making buying decisions unstrategically)
  • We solve it using backwards induction (as usual)

Pricing in Vertical Chains

  • Backward induction: start in last stage (Retailer)
  • $\Pi(p_R)=q(p-c)=(120-p_R)(p_R-p_W)$
  • FOC: $120-2p_R+p_W=0 \iff p_R=\dfrac{120+p_W}2$
  • This implies that demand will be $D(p_W)=120-p_R=\dfrac{120-p_W}2$

Pricing in Vertical Chains

  • Backward induction: stage 1
  • From stage 2, we know $D(p_W)=\dfrac{120-p_W}2$
  • $\Pi_W(p_W)=q(p-c)=\dfrac{120-p_W}2 (p_W-30)$
  • FOC: $\frac 12 (150-2p_W)=0 \iff p_W=75$
  • $p_R=\dfrac{120+p_W}2=97.5$
  • $D=\dfrac{120-p_W}2=22.5$
  • As we will see, this is a very bad outcome, even compared to regular monopoly

Pricing in Vertical Chains

  • Suppose the firms merge
  • $\Pi_s=q(p-c)=(120-p_S)(p_S-30)$
  • FOC: $150-2p_S=0 \iff p_S=75$
  • $D=120-p_S=45$
  • Prices much higher and quantities much lower with double marginalisation
  • "Only thing worse than a monopoly is a chain of monopolies"
  • Both consumers and producers can lose from double marginalisation

Entry with Fixed Costs

  • Week 1: price-taking equilibrium is efficient (and exists)
  • We mostly omitted production
  • For equilibrium with production, need additional assumption:
    • Convexity of production frontier ($\approx$ no weird jumps in cost of production)
  • Unfortunately, jumps in the costs of production are very natural: Fixed costs
  • This breaks the equilibrium model
    • price taking assumption cannot hold
    • Strategic models exist, but are inefficient

Fixed costs and Efficiency

  • Producing good has constant marginal cost $c$ and one time entry (start-up) cost $F$
  • Demand is $D(p)=S(1-p)$, where $S$ is the size of the market
  • Efficiency requires producing until the willingness to pay in the market decreases to $c$
  • I.e. until $p(Q)=1-\frac QS = c \iff Q=S(1-c)$
  • How many firms should enter into the market?
  • Only 1; more just adds to the fixed costs (because marginal cost is constant)

Fixed costs and the Market

  • Price taking market: Supply$=$Demand
  • Demand: $D(p)=S(1-p)>0$, if $c\lt1$
  • Supply:
    • $\Pi(Q)=Q(p-c)-F$
    • If $p>c$, supply is unbounded
    • If $p\lt c$, supply is 0
    • If $p=c$, supply is still zero! ↯
  • The market does not clear for any price: no equilibrium!
  • Natural monopoly: price-taking assumption cannot hold
  • Need models of trategic competition: Cournot

Fixed costs, Entry and Cournot

  • $D(p)=S(1-p) \iff p=1-\frac QS$
  • Production has marginal cost $c=0$ and fixed cost $F$
  • Two stage game
    1. Firms choose whether to enter (paying $F$) or not
    2. Entered firms set $q_i$
  • Denote $Q_{\neg i}$ the supply set by all firms other than firm $i$ and by $Q$ total production
  • Second stage (denote $n$ the number of firms that entered in stage 1)
    • $\Pi(q_i)=q_i(p-c)=q_i(1-\frac{q_i+Q_{\neg i}}S)$
    • FOC: $1-\frac{2q_i+Q_{\neg i}}S=0=1-\frac{q_i+Q}S$
    • This is true for all $n$ firms that entered
    • So we can sum over all $n$ firms
    • $0=n-\frac{Q+nQ}S \iff Q=\frac{nS}{n+1} \iff p=\frac1{n+1}$
    • Symmetry implies $q_i=\frac S{n+1}$

Fixed costs, Entry and Cournot

  • In stage 2: $p=\frac1{n+1}$ and $q_i=\frac S{n+1}$
  • $\Pi_i=q(p-c)-F=\frac S{(n+1)^2}-F$
  • Stage 1
    • Firms enter as long as profit is positive
    • $\Pi_i=\frac S{(n+1)^2}-F=0 \iff n=\sqrt{\frac SF}-1$
    • $n^*$ is the largest integer $n$ smaller than that
    • E.g. if $\sqrt{\frac SF}-1=3.456$, 3 firms enter

Entry, Cournot and Efficiency

  • We know efficiency requires $p=c$ and $n=1$
  • Cournot has $p>c$ and $n>1$
  • But increasing $n$ increases competition
  • As $S\uparrow$, $p\downarrow$ (good for efficiency) and $n\uparrow$ (bad for efficiency)
  • Perhaps surprisingly, second effect wins
  • Strategic competition not even "second-best"

Entry, Cournot and Efficiency

  • $D(p)=S(1-p)$
  • Welfare
    • $W=CS+PS-FC=\frac 12 (p_{max}-p^*)q^*+(p^*-c)q^*-nF$
    • $=\frac 12 S(1-p)^2 +S(1-p)p-nF$
    • $=\frac 12 S(1-\frac 1{(n+1)^2})-nF$
  • Optimal number of firms, given Cournot competition
    • FOC: $\dfrac S{(n+1)^3}-F=0 \iff n=\sqrt[3]{\frac SF}-1$
