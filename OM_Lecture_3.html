  • Last week (not quite): work in teams

Team production

  • When many work on a project, individual contributions are unclear
  • Rewards are usually based on total performance
  • Leads to free-rider problem
  • Simple model of team production:
    • Costs and benefits measured in money
    • If team members exert efforts $e_i$, $i=1,2,\ldots, n$
    • Quantity (sold at price $p$) is $e_1+\ldots+e_n$
    • Costs (individual) are $c_i(e_i)=\frac 12 e_i^2$

Efficient outcome

  • The value created is $V(e_1,\ldots,e_n)=p\cdot(e_1+\ldots+e_n)-(\frac 12 e_1^2+\ldots+\frac 12 e_n^2$)
  • Maximizing this value w.r.t. $e_i$
  • FOC: $p-e_i=0$, or $e^*=p$
  • Is efficient outcome unique?
    • Productive vs allocative efficiency

Nash of Team

  • What is the Nash equilibrium if every employee decides efforts individually and they split profits equally?
  • $\Pi_i(e_1,\ldots,e_n)=\frac 1n p\cdot(e_1+\ldots+e_n)-\frac 12 e_i^2$
  • FOC: $\frac 1n p - e_i=0$, or $e_i=\frac pn$
  • (This is a dominant strategy; extreme simplification)
  • $e_i\lt e^*$, and it gets worse as $n\uparrow$
  • Free-rider problem: your effort benefits everyone, you only work for your slice
  • Very bad underproduction (positive externalities)
